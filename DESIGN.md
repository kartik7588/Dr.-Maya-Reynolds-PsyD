## Overview

**Creative North Star: “Santa Monica Morning Room.”** The system feels like daylight moving across warm plaster, paper, walnut, and a view toward the Pacific. It is editorial rather than spa-like: high-contrast serif statements, a clear humanist sans, rectangular photographs, fine rules, and purposeful areas of open space create calm through composition instead of decorative softness.

The reusable identity is quiet, direct, and materially specific. Pacific Ink carries authority, Restrained Iris marks action, and the warm neutrals hold long-form reading. Real portrait and office photography supply the human presence. The homepage expresses this world through an asymmetric first viewport and alternating light and dark editorial fields; those route-specific arrangements should not become a template for every surface.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Sun-washed warm paper and plaster fields with Pacific Ink typography.
- High-contrast Cormorant Garamond statements paired with humanist Manrope copy.
- Asymmetric editorial composition, rectangular image crops, and hairline rules.
- Restrained iris accents and small walnut details rather than a scattered palette.
- Daylight is the only atmospheric motion material.

**The Morning Room Rule.** Every surface should feel naturally lit, calm, and composed; warmth comes from material color, photography, and space, never generic wellness decoration.

## Colors

Pacific Ink is the primary text and dark-field color. Warm Paper is the clean reading surface, while Sun-washed Plaster provides adjacent tonal separation. Restrained Iris is reserved for primary action, the final call-to-action field, small markers, and select italic emphasis. Walnut appears sparingly in hairlines or details. Coastal Mist and Iris Light support photographic settings and legibility on dark fields.

**The One Iris Rule.** Treat iris as a deliberate point of attention, not a color to distribute across every section.

**The Tonal Field Rule.** Separate major regions with full fields of paper, plaster, ink, or mist and with fine rules; do not simulate structure with card collections.

## Typography

Cormorant Garamond is the editorial voice for names, section headings, questions, and large statements. It uses medium weight, compressed leading, and modest negative tracking at display sizes. Manrope carries navigation, body copy, labels, and controls with generous line height and plainspoken clarity. Italic serif is a rare personal accent for the degree or practitioner name, not a general decorative style.

Headlines use balanced wrapping where supported. Body measures remain narrow enough for reflective reading, generally around 36–42rem, while display statements can span broader editorial columns.

**The Two Voices Rule.** Serif creates recognition and emotional cadence; sans-serif explains and directs. Do not reverse those jobs or introduce a third type personality.

## Layout

The page uses centered containers between roughly 73.75rem and 90rem, with 1.25rem mobile gutters, 2.5rem tablet gutters, and 4rem desktop gutters. Major sections breathe vertically at 6rem on small screens and 8rem on larger screens. Desktop compositions favor unequal two-column grids—often 0.7/1.3, 0.9/1.1, or 1.02/0.98—rather than symmetrical card grids.

On the homepage, the first viewport places the large promise on the left and a dominant portrait on the right, with a smaller office photograph overlapping its lower-left edge and a vertical marginal note. Subsequent sections preserve a clear story: recognition, who the practice helps, services, approach, practitioner, office, questions, then consultation. At tablet and mobile sizes, these grids become a single reading column; the vertical note disappears below 40rem and navigation becomes an explicit menu below the header rule.

**The Asymmetric Pair Rule.** Pair one dominant element with one supporting element—headline and portrait, image and text, label and content—rather than giving every column equal weight.

**The Open Floor Rule.** Use whitespace and hairline divisions to organize information. Avoid boxed modules unless the box is materially meaningful, such as a paper caption laid over a photograph.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is predominantly flat. Depth belongs to overlapping photography and the mobile navigation only, using broad, low-opacity ambient shadows. The office photograph may carry a barely perceptible rotation, like a physical print placed on a table. Content rows, FAQ items, and sections remain flush and depend on tonal contrast or hairline rules.

The hero’s diagonal sunbeam moves once over eighteen seconds with a slow ease-out. Hover motion is limited to the consultation arrow shifting slightly and service rows receiving an almost imperceptible lift in background tone. Reduced-motion preferences collapse all animation and transitions.

**The Daylight Only Rule.** Atmospheric motion must read as changing daylight; do not add floating objects, parallax layers, pulsing ornaments, or perpetual ambient loops.

## Shapes

Rectangles are the default form. Buttons, image crops, captions, fields, and menu panels use square corners. Fine one-pixel borders and dividers provide structure. The only recurring circle is the small iris bullet used to mark therapeutic modalities.

**The Unsoftened Edge Rule.** Preserve square corners and straight crops. Rounded wellness cards, pills, and blob masks contradict the system.

## Components

The primary consultation link is a 3rem-high rectangular iris control with white Manrope text and a right arrow. It shifts to Pacific Ink on hover; its light variant uses Warm Paper over dark or iris fields. Focus uses a two-pixel current-color outline offset by four pixels.

Primary navigation is quiet inline text beneath a full-width hairline header rule. The current action is indicated by a short underline, not a filled capsule. On mobile, navigation opens as a Warm Paper panel with one ruled row per destination.

Service entries are full-width editorial rows separated by translucent rules on a Pacific Ink field. FAQ entries follow the same ruled-row logic on Warm Paper; a serif question and plus icon form the trigger, the icon rotates 45 degrees when open, and the answer stays in a restrained reading measure.

Photography uses real supplied portrait and office images in rectangular, art-directed crops. Paper-colored borders, a paper caption, or a subtle ambient shadow may make a photograph feel placed in the room; imagery should never be converted into generic card thumbnails.

**The Row, Not Card Rule.** Repeated information belongs in divided editorial rows or open columns, not isolated raised cards.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Do use full tonal fields, asymmetrical columns, and hairline rules to establish hierarchy.
- Do keep copy calm, direct, specific, and easy to scan without reducing it to slogans.
- Do use the supplied portrait and office photography as primary narrative material.
- Do reserve serif scale for emotionally important statements and questions.
- Do preserve visible keyboard focus, semantic controls, descriptive image alternatives, and reduced-motion behavior.

### Don't

- Don't introduce generic wellness cards, rounded containers, floating badges, or decorative blobs.
- Don't scatter iris across minor elements or add unrelated accent colors.
- Don't use gradients as a substitute for daylight, photography, or material fields.
- Don't invent testimonials, outcomes, pricing, insurance details, contact information, or credentials.
- Don't turn the homepage's exact hero overlap or section sequence into a universal page template.
