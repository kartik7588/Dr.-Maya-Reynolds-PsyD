# Five-minute client walkthrough

## 0:00–0:40 — Welcome and goal

“Hi Maya, here’s the first draft of your new website. I designed it to help the kind of clients you described—capable, thoughtful adults who may look fine on the outside while feeling anxious, exhausted, or constantly on edge—feel understood within the first few seconds.”

## 0:40–1:25 — Hero and design direction

“The opening message is intentionally specific: therapy for anxiety, trauma, and burnout in Santa Monica. That gives visitors immediate clarity and helps local search visibility. The second line captures your combination of practical tools and deeper work. The warm plaster, deep blue, walnut, and natural-light palette comes directly from the calm feeling of your office rather than using a typical clinical blue or generic wellness aesthetic.”

## 1:25–2:10 — Empathy and services

“The next section reflects the experience many of your clients described: they keep functioning, but their mind and body rarely stand down. From there, the three main services come directly from your profile—anxiety and panic, trauma therapy, and burnout and perfectionism. Each description focuses on what a client may experience and how the work can help, without making unrealistic promises.”

## 2:10–2:55 — Approach and bio

“The approach section explains that insight and the nervous system do not always change at the same pace. I included the methods you named—CBT, EMDR, mindfulness, and body-oriented techniques—but kept the language accessible. Your bio then brings the relationship forward: collaborative, grounded, and tailored rather than one-size-fits-all.”

## 2:55–3:35 — Office section

“This office section is new and unique to your site. It uses your real office images and explains what clients can expect: privacy, natural light, and an uncluttered place to settle. It also includes your Santa Monica address and makes the choice between in-person and secure telehealth sessions easy to understand.”

## 3:35–4:15 — FAQs and next step

“The FAQs answer the questions a prospective client is most likely to have before contacting you: who you work with, where sessions happen, your therapeutic approach, how trauma work is paced, and what makes someone a good fit. The final invitation is calm and direct—there’s no pressure or exaggerated promise.”

## 4:15–4:50 — Mobile walkthrough

“On mobile, the same story remains intact. Navigation becomes a simple menu, the headline stays prominent, the portrait and office preview stack clearly, and service details remain easy to scan. Buttons are large enough to tap and the FAQ opens one answer at a time so the page never feels overwhelming.”

## 4:50–5:00 — Close

“Overall, the site is designed to feel like an extension of your work: clear enough to feel safe, warm enough to feel human, and thoughtful enough to communicate depth. I’d love to hear what feels most like you and what you’d want to refine next.”
